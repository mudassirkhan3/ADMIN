Topic 01 • Core Concept

Research Skills & Evidence-Based Practice

Think About This: Why do nurses change how they clean wounds or prevent infections over time? Because new research proves safer methods!

1. Best Research

Latest proven scientific studies

2. Nurse Expertise

Clinical skill & judgment

3. Patient Values

Patient needs & preferences

Practical Example: Using updated chlorhexidine protocols instead of plain water for ICU oral care reduces pneumonia rates.

Evidence-Based Practice (EBP) = Research + Clinical Skill + Patient Care
Topic 02 • Data Classification

Understanding Types of Data (Two Separate Ways)

Do not confuse where data comes from with what form it takes. They are two separate classifications:

A. By Source (Where it comes from)

  • Primary Data (First-Hand): Collected directly by you.
    Example: Asking 40 ward patients about their pain after surgery.
  • Secondary Data (Already Collected): Recorded earlier by others.
    Example: Checking last year's hospital infection records.

B. By Nature (What form it takes)

  • Quantitative Data (Numbers): Measured in digits & statistics.
    Example: Blood pressure ($120/80$ mmHg) or body temperature ($37^\circ\text{C}$).
  • Qualitative Data (Words): Feelings, stories & experiences.
    Example: Interviewing mothers about postpartum stress.
Competency 23 • Locate Credible Scientific Data[cite: 2, 3]

Reliable Scientific Databases for Nurses

Never rely on random blogs or social media for patient care. Use trusted medical search engines:

1. PubMed

Free database by the U.S. National Library of Medicine with over 36 million biomedical and clinical articles.

2. CINAHL

The top database made specifically for nursing and allied health journals and bedside care studies.

3. Cochrane Library

The highest authority for Systematic Reviews that summarize global proof on treatments.

4. Google Scholar

Easy-to-use academic search engine for finding peer-reviewed articles and checking how often they are cited.

Competency 23 • Quality Check[cite: 2, 3]

Evaluating Information with the CRAAP Test

C

Currency

Is it recent? (Published within last 5 years)

R

Relevance

Does it answer your nursing question?

A

Authority

Is the author a qualified nurse or doctor?

A

Accuracy

Are claims backed by real citations & data?

P

Purpose

Is it meant to educate or sell a product?

Nursing Example: A website claiming "Herbal tea cures diabetes in 2 days" with no doctor's name and a "Buy Now" button fails Authority, Accuracy, and Purpose.

Competency 23 • Smart Searching[cite: 2, 3]

Refining Searches with Boolean Operators

Use AND, OR, NOT in capital letters to control how many articles a database shows you:

AND (Narrows Search)

Nursing
Safety

Nursing AND Patient Safety
Shows only articles containing both words.

OR (Broadens Search)

Nurse
Nursing

Nurse OR Nursing
Shows articles with either word (good for synonyms).

NOT (Excludes Terms)

Infection
COVID

Infection NOT COVID-19
Removes unwanted topics from your results.

Competency 23 • Clinical Questions[cite: 2, 3]

Building Focused Questions with PICO

P

Population / Patient

Who are the patients?
e.g., Nursing students

I

Intervention

What action is tested?
e.g., Video training

C

Comparison

Compared to what?
e.g., Standard lecture

O

Outcome

What is the result?
e.g., Better hand hygiene

Complete PICO Question: "In nursing students (P), does video training (I) compared to a standard lecture (C) improve hand hygiene compliance (O)?"

Topic 07 • Study Approaches

Comparing Research Designs

A research design is your overall plan for answering your study question:

Research Design Main Purpose Type of Data Nursing Example
1. Quantitative Measure variables & test cause-and-effect Numbers & statistics Testing if a new mattress reduces bedsores (count of ulcers)
2. Qualitative Understand human feelings & lived experiences Words & interviews Interviewing nurses about emotional stress in the ICU
3. Mixed-Methods Get a complete picture using both approaches Both numbers & words Measuring exam scores (numbers) + asking students how they felt (words)
Competency 24 • Develop a Research Proposal[cite: 2, 3]

9 Essential Components of a Research Proposal

A research proposal is a written blueprint submitted for approval before you start collecting data:

1. TitleClear, concise name of your study
2. IntroductionBackground of the clinical issue
3. Problem StatementWhy this gap needs urgent study
4. ObjectivesSpecific, measurable study goals
5. Research QuestionsFocused questions (e.g., PICO)
6. Literature ReviewSummary of past scientific studies
7. MethodologyDesign, sample size & survey tools
8. Ethical ConsiderationsHow patient rights stay protected
9. ReferencesProper APA / Vancouver citations
Competency 24 • Step-by-Step Workflow[cite: 2, 3]

How to Develop Your Proposal Step-by-Step

  • Step 1 — Pick a Clinical Problem: Notice a real issue on the ward (e.g., needle-stick injuries).
  • Step 2 — Search Literature: Use PubMed & CINAHL to see what is already known.
  • Step 3 — Set Objectives & Design: Decide who you will study and what questionnaire you will use.
  • Step 4 — Plan Ethics & Timeline: Prepare your consent form and monthly work schedule.
  • Step 5 — Get Approval: Submit to your supervisor and Ethics Committee before starting.
Careful planning prevents mistakes during hospital data collection
Topic 09 • Patient Protection

Ethical Principles in Nursing Research

🤝 Informed Consent & Voluntary Choice

Explain risks and benefits clearly. Participants must join freely and can stop at any time without penalty.

🔒 Confidentiality & Privacy

Never publish patient names or hospital IDs. Use secret codes (e.g., Patient #01) to keep medical data private.

⚖️ Beneficence, Non-Maleficence & Justice

Do Good (beneficence), Do No Harm (non-maleficence), and treat all participants fairly (justice).

📝 Proper Referencing (No Plagiarism)

Never copy-paste another author's work. Always paraphrase in your own words and cite the original source.
