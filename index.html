Topic 1 • Foundations

Introduction to Pediatric Communication

  • What is Communication? A two-way exchange of thoughts, feelings, and information through words, tone, facial expressions, and body language.
  • What is Pediatric Communication? Adapting verbal and non-verbal interaction to match a child's developmental stage while partnering with their family.
  • Why Different from Adults? Children have limited vocabulary, concrete thinking, shorter attention spans, and express fear through behavior rather than words.
  • Why Age-Appropriate? Preschoolers take words literally; adolescents need privacy and respect. One style does not fit all ages.
  • Child + Family Partnership: Parents are a child's primary security—supporting parents calms the child and builds trust, comfort, safety, cooperation, and emotional support.

😰 Frightened, Unsupported Child

Feels overwhelmed by strangers and loud equipment; cries, clings to parents, or resists care.

😊 Supported, Reassured Child

Understands what to expect in simple terms, feels emotionally safe, trusts the nurse, and cooperates better.

Topic 1 • Practical Guidelines

12 Guidelines for Communicating with Children

  • 1. Approach the child calmly and respectfully.
  • 2. Introduce yourself clearly (name & role).
  • 3. Use the child's preferred name.
  • 4. Get down to the child's eye level when appropriate.
  • 5. Use simple, age-appropriate words.
  • 6. Speak slowly and clearly.
  • 7. Give the child time to respond.
  • 8. Use open-ended questions when they can understand.
  • 9. Use toys, pictures, gestures, and demonstrations.
  • 10. Be honest and avoid giving false promises.
  • 11. Respect the child's feelings and privacy.
  • 12. Encourage the child to express fears and questions.

✅ Nurse SHOULD Say:

"Hi Ali, I'm Nurse Sara. I'm going to listen to your chest with this stethoscope—it feels a little cool. Would you like to touch it first?"

❌ Nurse SHOULD AVOID Saying:

"Hold still right now! I'm going to take your vitals and give you a shot—it won't hurt at all."

Topic 1 • Developmental Timeline

Communication According to Age & Development

👶 1. Infants
(0–12 Mos)

  • Gentle, soothing voice.
  • Warm facial expressions.
  • Gentle touch when appropriate.
  • Comforting presence & parent closeness.
Ex: Softly humming while parent holds infant during exam.

🚶 2. Toddlers
(1–3 Yrs)

  • Short, simple sentences.
  • Demonstrate on dolls/toys.
  • Give simple choices.
  • Allow comfort objects.
Ex: "Blue bandage or star bandage?" while holding teddy.

🎨 3. Preschool
(3–5 Yrs)

  • Concrete, literal words.
  • Simple explanations.
  • Stories & pretend play.
  • Avoid frightening terms.
Ex: Say "clean the skin" instead of "cut or stick."

🎒 4. School-Age
(6–12 Yrs)

  • Explain what will happen.
  • Encourage questions.
  • Use pictures & demos.
  • Allow participation.
Ex: Show diagram of lungs & let child hold spacer.

🧑‍🎓 5. Adolescents
(13–18 Yrs)

  • Respect privacy.
  • Show respect & honesty.
  • Involve in decisions.
  • Avoid judgment; support autonomy.
Ex: Speak privately when appropriate & ask their goals.

Topic 1 • Family-Centered Care

Communicating Effectively with Families

👨‍👩‍👧 Key Family Communication Principles

  • Listen actively to parents and caregivers.
  • Show genuine empathy and patience.
  • Respect cultural beliefs and family values.
  • Give clear info; avoid unnecessary medical jargon.
  • Encourage questions and provide realistic reassurance.
  • Include families in care and protect confidentiality.
  • Recognize parental stress and anxiety.

📌 Clinical Scenario:

A mother is visibly anxious because her 3-year-old child needs IV therapy.

❌ Poor Communication:

"Don't worry, we insert cannulas all day. Just step outside while we start the IV line."

✅ Professional Nursing Response:

"I understand this feels scary. Your child needs fluids through a tiny soft tube in the hand so they feel stronger. You can stay beside them and hold their other hand. What questions do you have?"

Topic 1 • Clinical Best Practices

Pediatric Communication: Do's & Don'ts

✅ DO (Supportive Practice)

  • Listen carefully without interrupting.
  • Use simple, honest language.
  • Show empathy and use a calm tone.
  • Give appropriate, safe choices.
  • Encourage questions and validate feelings.

❌ DON'T (Harmful Habits)

  • Threaten or shame the child for crying.
  • Use frightening words (e.g., "cut," "shoot").
  • Ignore the child's questions.
  • Make false promises ("It won't hurt at all").
  • Rush unnecessarily or talk over the child.

Instead of: "Stop crying, it doesn't hurt."  ➡️  Say: "I can see you're scared. I'll explain what we're going to do, and you can hold your teddy bear."

Topic 2 • Child Development

Role of Play in Growth & Development

What is Play? Play is a child's natural, voluntary activity for exploring the world, learning skills, and expressing thoughts. It is essential for healthy childhood development across all five domains:

🏃 Physical

Builds gross & fine motor strength, balance, and coordination.
Ex: Running, active play, stacking blocks.

🧠 Cognitive

Develops problem-solving, memory, cause-and-effect, and focus.
Ex: Building blocks, puzzles.

🗣️ Language

Expands vocabulary, storytelling, and two-way communication.
Ex: Singing songs, puppet dialogue.

💖 Emotional

Allows safe release of fear, anger, and stress while building confidence.
Ex: Drawing feelings, pretend play.

🤝 Social

Teaches sharing, empathy, cooperation, and taking turns.
Ex: Playing group games with peers.

Topic 2 • Developmental Progression

7 Types of Play in Child Development

1. Solitary Play

Plays alone with own toys (Infants). Ex: Shaking a rattle.
Value: Sensory exploration & focus.

2. Parallel Play

Plays beside peers without sharing (Toddlers). Ex: Two toddlers stacking blocks side-by-side.
Value: Peer comfort & observation.

3. Associative Play

Shares toys without formal rules (Preschool). Ex: Sharing crayons while drawing.
Value: Sharing & social language.

4. Cooperative Play

Organized group play with rules/roles (School-age). Ex: Board games or team sports.
Value: Teamwork & rule following.

5. Pretend / Dramatic Play

Acting out roles & imagination (Preschool+). Ex: Playing "doctor/nurse" with a doll.
Value: Emotional processing & empathy.

6. Constructive Play

Building or creating objects (Toddlers–School-age). Ex: Lego towers or clay modeling.
Value: Fine motor & problem-solving.

7. Physical / Active Play

Whole-body movement (All ages). Ex: Hopping, ball toss, gentle hall walk.
Value: Strength, energy release & coordination.

Topic 3 • Hospitalized Child

10 Functions of Play for Hospitalized Children

  • 1. Reduces anxiety and fear.
  • 2. Provides safe emotional expression.
  • 3. Maintains a sense of normalcy.
  • 4. Reduces hospital boredom.
  • 5. Promotes social interaction.
  • 6. Improves psychological coping.
  • 7. Helps children understand procedures.
  • 8. Increases cooperation with nurses.
  • 9. Supports ongoing developmental needs.
  • 10. Gives a sense of control and choice.

❌ Without Supportive Play

Leads to heightened fear, isolation, boredom, regression, and emotional withdrawal.

✅ With Appropriate Play

Promotes comfort, emotional expression, engagement, and improved coping—while recognizing play supports care rather than replacing medical pain relief.

Topic 4 • Core Nursing Intervention

Therapeutic Play in Pediatric Nursing

🧸 What is Therapeutic Play?

  • Definition: Structured, purposeful play guided by a nurse to help a child cope with illness, hospitalization, and medical procedures.
  • Purpose: Prepares children for procedures, lets them express fears, reduces stress, and restores a feeling of control.
  • How Nurses Use It: Integrated into bedside assessments, pre-procedure teaching, and emotional support.
  • When Useful: Before injections, dressing changes, surgery, oxygen therapy, or prolonged admissions.

🩺 Practical Nursing Examples

  • 💉 Letting a child practice a pretend injection on a doll.
  • 🧰 Exploring a safe toy medical kit (stethoscope, mask).
  • 🫧 Blowing bubbles before or during a stressful procedure.
  • 🖍️ Drawing feelings about being in the hospital.
  • 🧸 Using hand puppets to explain upcoming treatment.
  • 🩹 Touching safe, sterile gauze or tubing before use.

Topic 4 • Key Clinical Distinction

Therapeutic Play vs. Play Therapy

Important Distinction: Therapeutic play is a supportive nursing care tool—it is not the same as formal psychological play therapy.

🩺 Therapeutic Play

  • Often used by pediatric nurses.
  • Part of routine bedside nursing care.
  • Supports coping, preparation, expression, and understanding.
  • Involves dolls, toys, medical kits, drawing, or games.
  • Focuses on a specific healthcare-related need.
  • Example: Nurse uses a teddy bear to show how a nebulizer mask works.

🧠 Play Therapy

  • A specialized therapeutic intervention.
  • Conducted by trained mental-health or certified play therapists.
  • Uses play within a formal psychotherapeutic relationship.
  • Addresses deeper emotional, behavioral, or psychological difficulties.
  • Requires specialized clinical training and psychological assessment.
  • Example: Weekly counseling sessions for severe childhood trauma.

Topic 5 • Procedural Care

Using Play Before, During & After Procedures

🕒 Before Procedure

  • Explain using simple pictures or toys.
  • Demonstrate steps on a doll first.
  • Let the child ask questions freely.
  • Offer suitable choices (e.g., which arm or sticker).

⚡ During Procedure

  • Use active distraction (bubbles, pinwheel, counting).
  • Encourage slow breathing games.
  • Allow a favorite comfort object.
  • Maintain calm, supportive communication and clinical safety.

🌟 After Procedure

  • Praise coping and effort honestly.
  • Encourage expression of feelings.
  • Provide comfort and parent reassurance.
  • Allow calming play and evaluate response.

⚠️ Clinical Safety Note: Therapeutic play enhances coping, but it never replaces prescribed analgesia (pain relief), informed consent/assent, aseptic technique, or clinical safety precautions.

Interactive Case Study

Clinical Case Activity: 4-Year-Old Dressing Change

🩹 Scenario: "A 4-year-old child needs a dressing change. The child is crying, frightened and refusing to allow the nurse near the wound."

💬 Class Discussion Prompts:

  1. How should the nurse communicate with the child?
  2. Which type of play could be used?
  3. How can the nurse prepare the child?
  4. What can the nurse do to reduce fear?
  5. How can the parents help?
  6. How should the nurse evaluate if the approach helped?
  • 1. Communicate: Sit at eye level, speak softly, use concrete words ("put a clean bandage on"), and avoid scary words.
  • 2. Type of Play: Dramatic / therapeutic medical play using a doll and bandage kit.
  • 3. Prepare: Demonstrate removing and placing a clean dressing on the doll first.
  • 4. Reduce Fear: Let the child hold a chosen toy, blow bubbles during removal, and ensure prescribed analgesia is given.
  • 5. Parents' Role: Parent holds the child's hand, offers familiar comfort, and reinforces calm praise.
  • 6. Evaluate: Observe decreased crying, relaxed body posture, cooperation, and verbal comfort afterward.

Unit IV Wrap-Up

Summary, Key Takeaways & References

📌 7 Key Takeaways

  • 1. Communicate according to the child's age, developmental level, and needs.
  • 2. Include and support parents and families as care partners.
  • 3. Play is essential for physical, cognitive, language, emotional, and social growth.
  • 4. Play helps hospitalized children cope, express feelings, and maintain normalcy.
  • 5. Therapeutic play is a vital nursing tool at the bedside.
  • 6. Nurses use play to prepare, educate, distract, and comfort children.
  • 7. Therapeutic play is distinct from formal psychological play therapy.

"For a child, play is more than fun—it is a language of learning, healing and trust."
