Locating Credible Scientific Data: Deep Dive

Understanding what credible data means and why it protects patients.

What Does It Mean?

Locating credible scientific data means finding accurate, trustworthy, and evidence-based information from reliable scientific sources rather than unverified personal opinions or social media rumors.

Safe Patient Care

Protects patients by grounding treatments in validated scientific proof.

Clinical Confidence

Empowers nurses with solid evidence for complex bedside decisions.

Filters Misinformation

Eliminates medical myths and unsupported health claims.

Competency 23 Sources

Primary vs. Secondary Scientific Sources

Distinguishing between first-hand research evidence and synthesized analysis.

Primary Sources

Original Research Evidence

Provide original, first-hand data collected directly by researchers.

  • Original research articles & clinical trials
  • Surveys, questionnaires, & interviews
  • Direct experimental studies
Nursing Example: Surveying hand hygiene compliance among BSN students.

Secondary Sources

Synthesized Research Analysis

Summarize, interpret, or analyze findings from previously published studies.

  • Systematic reviews & meta-analyses
  • Literature review articles
  • Research textbooks
Nursing Example: Reading a systematic review summarizing 15 infection control studies.

Reliable Information

  • Written by qualified authors in peer-reviewed journals.
  • Backed by clear scientific evidence and citations.

Unreliable Information

  • Anonymous social media posts with zero references.
  • Sensationalized, emotional personal opinions.
Competency 24 Focus

Developing a Research Proposal: What & Why

Understanding the blueprint of scientific investigation.

What Is a Research Proposal?

A structured written plan explaining WHAT you want to study, WHY the study is clinically important, and HOW the research will be executed.

Think of it as the navigation roadmap guiding a researcher before entering the clinical field.

Core Purposes of a Proposal

Clearly define the clinical problem
Establish precise objectives & research questions
Plan robust methodology and sampling
Justify clinical significance and value
Obtain ethical/IRB approval prior to study

Competency 24 Methodology

Steps to Develop a Proposal & Nursing Example

Moving from an initial idea to a completed research plan.

1 Select relevant nursing research topic
2 Identify clinical problem clearly
3 Review scientific literature thoroughly
4 Formulate objectives & research questions
5 Choose appropriate research design
6 Determine study population & sample
7 Plan data collection and analysis
8 Address ethical considerations & IRB
9 Compile references & finalize document
Practical Nursing Example

Topic: Hand Hygiene Compliance Among Nursing Students.

Problem: Inconsistent adherence to hygiene protocols.

Objective: To assess compliance levels among students.

Research Question: What is the level of compliance among BSN students?

Methodology: Cross-sectional observational study using audit checklists.

Importance: Findings improve hospital infection control.

Summary & Review

Key Takeaways of Research Skills

Core lessons connecting both competencies for professional nursing.

1. Evidence Over Opinion

Always rely on verified primary/secondary scientific literature from databases like PubMed rather than unverified anecdotes.

2. Critical Screening

Use the CRAP Test to rigorously evaluate online health information before applying it in patient care settings.

3. Structured Planning

A research proposal provides an indispensable 9-step blueprint ensuring ethical integrity, clear questions, and rigorous methods.
