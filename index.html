Competency 23

Locate Credible Scientific Data

Mastering the foundation of trustworthy health information discovery.

What Does It Mean?

Finding accurate, trustworthy, and peer-reviewed scientific evidence from reliable databases rather than unverified personal opinions.

Safe Patient Care

Grounds clinical actions in proven scientific facts.

Clinical Decisions

Provides strong evidence for complex bedside choices.

Prevent Misinformation

Filters out health myths and unverified claims.

Two Main Types of Scientific Sources:

1. Primary Sources (Original Research) 2. Secondary Sources (Synthesized Analysis)
Source Classification

Primary vs. Secondary Sources & Databases

Primary Sources

Original Research Evidence

First-hand information collected directly by researchers.

  • Original research articles & clinical trials
  • Surveys, questionnaires, & interviews
  • Direct experimental studies
Nursing Example: Surveying hand hygiene rates among BSN students.

Secondary Sources

Existing Research Analysis

Summaries or interpretations of previously published studies.

  • Systematic reviews & meta-analyses
  • Literature review articles
  • Research textbooks
Nursing Example: Reading a systematic review on infection control.

Relevance Evaluation

  • Does it directly answer your research question?
  • Is the academic depth appropriate?

Authority Evaluation

  • Who is the author or publisher?
  • Are they qualified experts in healthcare?

P – Purpose

Purpose Evaluation

  • Why was it published (educate, sell, persuade)?
  • Is there evident bias?
Key Reminder: The CRAP Test is a quick screening tool, not an absolute guarantee of scientific validity.
Critical Appraisal

Reliable vs. Unreliable Information in Nursing

Reliable Information

  • Written by qualified healthcare researchers.
  • Published in peer-reviewed journals.
  • Supported by rigorous citations and data.
  • Transparent, reproducible methods.

Unreliable Information

  • Anonymous social media posts & blogs.
  • Lacks scientific references.
  • Personal opinions presented as medical facts.
  • Outdated or sensationalized claims.
Practical Nursing Example: Hand Hygiene & Hospital Infections
1. Search: PubMed 2. Select: Peer-reviewed trial 3. Verify: Author & references 4. Apply: Safe clinical practice
Key Message: Never accept unverified online health claims without checking credibility!
Competency 24

Develop a Research Proposal

Understanding the scientific investigation blueprint.

What Is a Research Proposal?

A structured written plan explaining WHAT you want to study, WHY it matters, and HOW you will conduct the research.

Acts as the roadmap guiding researchers before starting empirical data collection.

Core Purposes of a Proposal

Define the clinical problem clearly
Establish research objectives & questions
Outline robust methodology & sampling
Justify clinical importance
Obtain ethical/IRB approval prior to study
Proposal Structure

Nine Essential Components of a Research Proposal

01

Title

Clear name of the study.

02

Introduction

Sets background context.

03

Problem Statement

Core issue investigated.

04

Objectives

Aims to be achieved.

05

Research Questions

Specific questions answered.

06

Literature Review

Synthesis of prior work.

07

Methodology

Design, tools & analysis.

08

Ethical Considerations

Participant rights & safety.

09

References

Credible sources cited.

Methodological Steps

From Research Idea to Final Proposal

1 Select relevant nursing topic
2 Identify clinical problem
3 Review scientific literature
4 Formulate objectives & questions
5 Choose appropriate design
6 Determine population & sample
7 Plan data collection & analysis
8 Address ethical considerations
9 Finalize proposal & references
Practical Nursing Example

Topic: Hand Hygiene Compliance Among Nursing Students.

Problem: Inconsistent adherence to hygiene protocols.

Objective: To assess compliance levels among students.

Research Question: What is the compliance level?

Methodology: Cross-sectional observational study.

Importance: Improves hospital infection control.

Summary & Review

Key Takeaways of Research Skills

Core lessons for every professional nursing student.

1. Evidence Over Opinion

Always rely on verified primary and secondary scientific sources from trusted databases like PubMed rather than unverified anecdotes.

2. Critical Evaluation

Use the CRAP Test (Currency, Relevance, Authority, Purpose) to screen online healthcare information before clinical application.

3. Structured Planning

A research proposal acts as an indispensable 9-step roadmap ensuring ethical standards, clear questions, and rigorous methodology.
