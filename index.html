Competency 23

Locate Credible Scientific Data

Mastering the foundation of trustworthy health information discovery.

What Does It Mean?

Locating credible scientific data means finding accurate, trustworthy, and evidence-based information from reliable scientific sources rather than unverified opinion.

Safe Patient Care

Protects patients by grounding clinical interventions in validated scientific proof.

Clinical Decisions

Empowers nurses with robust evidence for complex bedside decisions.

Prevent Misinformation

Filters out clinical myths, social media rumors, and unsupported claims.

Two Main Types of Scientific Sources:

1. Primary Sources (Original Research) 2. Secondary Sources (Synthesized Analysis)
Source Classification

Primary vs. Secondary Sources & Databases

Primary Sources

Original Research Evidence

Provide original, first-hand information collected or reported by researchers.

  • Original research articles & clinical trials
  • Surveys, questionnaires, & interviews
  • Direct observations & experimental studies
Nursing Example: Conducting a survey to assess hand hygiene practices among BSN nursing students.

Secondary Sources

Existing Research Analysis

Summarize, interpret, or analyze findings from previously published research.

  • Systematic reviews & meta-analyses
  • Literature reviews & review articles
  • Research textbooks
Nursing Example: Reading a systematic review summarizing 15 studies on hand hygiene & infection control.

Evaluation Framework

The CRAP Test for Online Information

C – Currency

Currency Evaluation

  • When was the information published or posted?
  • Has it been recently revised or updated?
  • Is the timeframe recent enough for your specific nursing topic?

R – Relevance

Click to expand

Relevance Evaluation

  • Does the information directly relate to your research question?
  • Is the depth and tone appropriate for academic research?
  • Did you examine alternative sources before selecting?

A – Authority

Click to expand

Authority Evaluation

  • Who is the author, creator, or publisher?
  • What are their credentials, institutional affiliations, or degrees?
  • Is the source peer-reviewed and reputable in healthcare?

P – Purpose

Click to expand

Purpose Evaluation

  • Why was the information published (educate, inform, persuade, sell)?
  • Is there evident political, corporate, or emotional bias?
  • Are the opinions objective and supported by data?
Key Reminder: The CRAP Test is a vital screening tool, not an absolute guarantee of scientific validity.
Critical Appraisal

Reliable vs. Unreliable Information in Nursing

Reliable Information

  • Written by qualified healthcare researchers/authors.
  • Published in peer-reviewed medical journals.
  • Supported by rigorous scientific evidence & citations.
  • Transparent methodology and reproducible results.

Unreliable Information

  • Anonymous social media posts & blogs.
  • Lacks scientific references or peer review.
  • Personal opinions or anecdotal claims presented as facts.
  • Misleading, sensationalized, or outdated health claims.
Practical Nursing Example: Hand Hygiene & Hospital Infections
1. Search: PubMed / CINAHL 2. Select: Peer-reviewed articles 3. Verify: Authors, dates, references 4. Apply: Evidence-based practice
Key Message: Never accept online medical information without verifying its credibility!
Competency 24

Develop a Research Proposal

Understanding the blueprint of scientific investigation.

What Is a Research Proposal?

A research proposal is a structured written plan that meticulously explains WHAT the researcher wants to study, WHY the study is important, and HOW the research will be conducted.

Think of it as the navigation roadmap that guides a researcher before starting any empirical study.

Core Purposes of a Research Proposal

Identify and define the research problem clearly
Establish precise research objectives & questions
Plan robust research methodology & sampling
Justify the clinical significance and importance
Obtain ethical & institutional approval before data collection
Proposal Structure

01

02

Sets background context.

03

Core issue investigated.

04

Objectives

Aims to be achieved.

05

Research Questions

Specific questions answered.

06

Literature Review

Synthesis of prior work.

07

Methodology

Design, tools & analysis.

08

Ethical Considerations

Participant rights & safety.

09

References

Credible sources cited.

Methodological Steps

From Research Idea to Final Proposal

1 Select a relevant nursing research topic
2 Identify the clinical research problem
3 Review scientific literature thoroughly
4 Formulate objectives and research questions
5 Choose appropriate research design
6 Determine study population and sampling
7 Plan data collection and analysis methods
8 Address ethical considerations & IRB approval
9 Compile references and finalize proposal document
Practical Nursing Example

Topic: Hand Hygiene Compliance Among Nursing Students.

Problem: Some students may not consistently follow proper hand hygiene protocols.

Objective: To assess hand hygiene compliance levels among nursing students.

Research Question: What is the level of compliance among nursing students?

Methodology: Descriptive cross-sectional study using validated observation checklists.

Importance: Findings enhance hospital infection prevention strategies.
