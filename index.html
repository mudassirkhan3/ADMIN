Subtopic 01 • Competency 23

What is Credible Scientific Data?

  • Peer-Reviewed Rigor: Evaluated blindly by independent medical and nursing experts before publication.
  • Reproducible Methodology: Clear sample size, inclusion criteria, and transparent statistical analysis.
  • Currency & Relevance: Published within the last 5 years to reflect current clinical standards.
  • Unbiased & Ethical: Free from commercial manipulation, backed by Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval.
Peer-reviewed nursing journals form the backbone of clinical truth
Subtopic 01 • Where to Search

Trusted Scientific Databases for Nurses

Avoid random blogs or unverified social media posts—use indexed health repositories.

1. PubMed / MEDLINE

Premier free biomedical database by the U.S. National Library of Medicine with over 36 million citations and clinical trials.

2. CINAHL (Nursing & Allied Health)

The gold-standard database dedicated specifically to nursing journals, bedside care procedures, and qualitative nursing studies.

3. Cochrane Library

Highest authority for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses that synthesize global evidence on clinical treatments.

4. Google Scholar & PakMediNet

Useful for broad citation tracking (Google Scholar) and accessing Pakistani medical & nursing research articles (PakMediNet).

Subtopic 01 • Precision Searching

Search Like a Scientist: PICO & Boolean Operators

P
Patient / Problem

e.g., ICU ventilated patients

I
Intervention

e.g., Chlorhexidine oral rinse

C
Comparison

e.g., Standard saline care

O
Outcome

e.g., Reduced VAP pneumonia

AND (Narrows Search)

"Preeclampsia" AND "Magnesium Sulfate" — Retrieves articles containing both terms.

OR (Broadens Search)

"Heart Attack" OR "Myocardial Infarction" — Captures synonyms and related terms.

NOT (Excludes Noise)

"Diabetes" NOT "Type 1" — Filters out irrelevant populations from your results.

Subtopic 01 • Quality Appraisal

How to Verify Source Credibility

  • Level I (Strongest): Systematic Reviews & Meta-Analyses of Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs).
  • Level II–III: Individual RCTs and Quasi-Experimental clinical studies.
  • Level IV–V: Cohort, Case-Control, and Qualitative descriptive nursing studies.
  • CRAAP Filter: Always verify Currency (date), Relevance, Authority (author credentials), Accuracy (references), and Purpose.
Critical appraisal protects patients from flawed or predatory studies
A strong proposal is the architectural blueprint of successful research
Subtopic 02 • Competency 24

What is a Research Proposal?

A formal, structured written plan submitted before data collection begins to prove that your study is scientifically valid, feasible, and ethical.

  • What clinical problem will be investigated?
  • Why does this gap in nursing knowledge matter?
  • How will participants be sampled and data analyzed safely?
Subtopic 02 • Structure (Front Half)

Core Components of a Proposal: Chapters 1 & 2

1. Concise Title & Introduction

Clear variables, target population, and clinical setting (e.g., "Prevalence of Needle-Stick Injuries Among Student Nurses in Multan").

2. Problem Statement & Objectives

Quantifies the clinical gap and sets SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) objectives.

3. Literature Review & Conceptual Framework

Synthesizes recent studies to justify why new research is needed and maps variable relationships.

Clear problem statements focus the entire investigation
Subtopic 02 • Structure (Back Half)

Core Components: Methodology, Timeline & Budget

🔬 Research Design & Setting

Specifies Cross-sectional, Descriptive, Cohort, or Experimental design, plus hospital ward or community setting.

👥 Population & Sampling Technique

Defines inclusion/exclusion criteria, sample size calculation, and probability vs. non-probability sampling.

📊 Data Collection Tools & Analysis

Validated questionnaires, clinical checklists, pilot testing, and statistical analysis plan (e.g., SPSS tests).

📅 Gantt Chart, Budget & References

Month-by-month work schedule, resource estimation, and standardized APA 7th or Vancouver citations.

Subtopic 02 • Protection of Human Subjects

Ethical Pillars in Nursing Research

  • IRB / Ethical Committee Approval: Mandatory institutional review before approaching a single patient.
  • Informed Consent: Voluntary participation after explaining risks, benefits, and the right to withdraw anytime.
  • Confidentiality & Anonymity: Coding patient identities so personal medical records remain strictly private.
  • Beneficence & Non-Maleficence: Maximizing clinical benefit while ensuring zero physical or psychological harm.
Patient dignity and safety always outweigh scientific curiosity
Integration & Best Practices

Connecting Competencies 23 & 24 — Avoiding Pitfalls

❌ Common Student Pitfalls

  • Citing Wikipedia, blogs, or 15-year-old textbooks instead of recent peer-reviewed articles.
  • Writing overly broad research titles with vague, unmeasurable objectives.
  • Copy-pasting text (plagiarism) or skipping pilot testing of questionnaires.

✅ Group 5 Golden Rules

  • Use Competency 23 (PubMed/CINAHL) to find gaps that justify your proposal.
  • Align title, objectives, questionnaire items, and statistical tests one-to-one.
  • Run plagiarism checks and secure formal IRB approval early.
