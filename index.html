Competency 23 Focus

Locating Credible Scientific Data

Unpacking the art and science of finding trustworthy health information.

Core Meaning & Definition

Locating credible data means systematically retrieving accurate, unbiased, and peer-reviewed scientific evidence from reputable databases, ensuring zero compromise on patient care.

Safe Interventions

Protects patients by anchoring treatments in verified scientific facts.

Bedside Confidence

Guides nurses through complex clinical dilemmas with robust data.

Eliminating Myths

Filters out internet rumors and unverified anecdotal medical advice.

Source Classification

Primary vs. Secondary Scientific Sources

Primary Sources

Original Research Evidence

First-hand empirical data collected directly by researchers.

  • Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs)
  • Surveys, questionnaires, & interviews
  • Direct clinical observations
Nursing Example: Surveying hospital hand hygiene compliance.

Secondary Sources

Synthesized Research Analysis

Evaluations and summaries of multiple primary studies.

  • Systematic reviews & meta-analyses
  • Critical literature review papers
  • Medical & nursing textbooks
Nursing Example: Reading a review summarizing 20 infection control trials.
Access Channels

Where to Find Credible Scientific Data

Navigating premier biomedical search engines and global health authorities.

Essential Research Databases & Global Agencies

PubMedNIH biomedical repository
Google ScholarScholarly articles & papers
CINAHLNursing & allied health index
WHOGlobal health reports & stats

Crucial Distinction: PubMed, Google Scholar, and CINAHL are search databases providing access to literature, whereas WHO is an international health organization publishing official guidelines.

Tips for Effective Database Searching

Use Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) and specific medical subject headings (MeSH terms) to narrow down your search results to high-impact peer-reviewed journals.

  • Is it recent enough for fast-evolving medical guidelines?

Relevance Evaluation

  • Does it directly answer your clinical question?
  • Is the academic depth suitable for nursing research?

A – Authority

Authority Evaluation

  • Who is the author or publisher?
  • Do they possess verified healthcare credentials?

P – Purpose

Purpose Evaluation

  • Why was it published (educate, sell, persuade)?
  • Is there commercial or ideological bias?
Key Reminder: The CRAP Test is a quick screening filter, not an absolute guarantee of clinical validity.
Critical Appraisal

Reliable vs. Unreliable Healthcare Information

Reliable Information

  • Authored by credentialed health researchers.
  • Published in peer-reviewed medical journals.
  • Backed by verifiable scientific citations.
  • Transparent, reproducible study methods.

Unreliable Information

  • Anonymous blogs and social media posts.
  • Total lack of scientific references.
  • Personal opinions marketed as medical cures.
  • Outdated or sensationalized health claims.
Clinical Appraisal Workflow
1. Search: PubMed / CINAHL 2. Filter: Peer-reviewed 3. Appraise: Use CRAP Test 4. Apply: Evidence-Based Nursing
Competency 24 Focus

Developing a Research Proposal

Mastering the roadmap of scientific investigation.

What Is a Research Proposal?

A structured written document that details WHAT you intend to investigate, WHY the study is clinically significant, and HOW you will execute the research.

Acts as an essential navigation blueprint guiding investigators prior to launching empirical data collection.

Core Purposes of a Proposal

Clearly define the clinical problem
Formulate precise objectives & questions
Outline robust methodology & sampling
Justify healthcare significance
Secure ethical / IRB approval
Proposal Structure (1 to 5)

First Five Components of a Research Proposal

01

Title

Concise, clear name reflecting the core variables of the study.

02

Introduction

Background context setting up the clinical background.

03

Problem Statement

Specific healthcare gap or issue being investigated.

04

Objectives

Broad aims and specific goals the study aims to achieve.

05

Research Questions

Precise inquiries the researcher seeks to answer.

★

Structural Note

These initial sections build the foundational rationale for your study.

Proposal Structure (6 to 9)

Remaining Components & Execution Plan

06

Literature Review

Synthesis of prior scientific work and evidence.

07

Methodology

Study design, population, sampling, tools & analysis plan.

08

Ethical Considerations

Informed consent, participant privacy, safety & IRB clearance.

09

References

Credible scientific sources cited in APA format.

✓

Ready for Review

A complete 9-component proposal ensures flawless institutional review.

Methodological Application

From Research Idea to Final Proposal

1 Select relevant nursing research topic
2 Identify clinical problem
3 Review scientific literature thoroughly
4 Formulate objectives & questions
5 Choose appropriate research design
6 Determine study population & sample
7 Plan data collection & analysis methods
8 Address ethical considerations & IRB
9 Compile references & finalize proposal
Practical Nursing Example

Topic: Hand Hygiene Compliance Among Nursing Students.

Problem: Inconsistent adherence to hygiene protocols.

Objective: To assess compliance levels among students.

Research Question: What is the level of compliance?

Methodology: Descriptive cross-sectional observational study.

Importance: Enhances hospital infection control standards.

Summary & Review

Key Takeaways for Professional Nurses

Essential principles for academic and clinical excellence.

1. Evidence Over Opinion

Always rely on verified primary and secondary scientific literature from trusted databases rather than unverified anecdotes.

2. Rigorous Screening

Apply the CRAP Test (Currency, Relevance, Authority, Purpose) to screen healthcare information before bedside application.

3. Structured Planning

A research proposal serves as an indispensable 9-step roadmap guaranteeing ethical standards and methodological rigor.
